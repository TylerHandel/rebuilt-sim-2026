# Real CAD in the game

Put CAD exports in this folder and list them in `manifest.json`. The game loads them at startup.
Physics always uses the colliders built in `js/field.js`; CAD is visual. They come from the game-manual dimensions, and the TRENCHES' shape (the sloped skirt, the 6 in arm and the AprilTag mount on it) was measured off this CAD so the colliders match what you see.

## The field
`field/field.glb` is the official field CAD (Onshape document 8a691e28680da30504859fce, "FE-2026: REBUILT
Playing Field"). Its GLB export is in meters, Z-up, with the blue alliance on +X. The manifest entry's
`rotationDeg: [-90, 0, 180]` maps that to the game's axes: Y-up, blue at −X. Refresh it with
`tools/onshape-export.sh` and then `tools/slim-glb.mjs` (see the main README).

## Getting the files
- **Onshape** (FIRST field, team robots): right-click the assembly tab → Export → **glTF (.glb)**, or STEP.
- **STEP**: convert here with `python3 tools/cad2glb.py cad/field/field.step cad/field/field.glb`
  (`pip install cadquery-ocp trimesh scipy`; add `--deflection 5` for fewer triangles).
- GitHub rejects files over 100 MB: export elements separately or use Git LFS.

## manifest.json
```json
{
  "models": [
    { "file": "field/field.glb", "kind": "field", "scale": 0.001, "rotationDeg": [-90, 0, 0], "position": [0, 0, 0] }
  ]
}
```
- `kind`: `"field"` replaces the drawn field (carpet kept); `"element"` just adds the model.
- `scale`: 0.001 for millimeters, 0.0254 for inches, 1 for meters.
- `rotationDeg`: converts CAD axes to the game's (Y up); `[-90, 0, 0]` suits Z-up CAD.
- `position`: meters; the game's origin is the field center, blue alliance wall at −X.
- `color` (optional): one color for the whole model if the export has none.
- `enabled` (optional): `false` skips the model.
- Drawn meshes flagged `userData.keepWithCad` (bleachers, HUMAN PLAYERS, HUB lights, CHUTE DOORS) stay visible under a `"field"` model.

## Robot parts
`robots/2910-intake.glb` is 2910's "Pivoting Intake Assembly" from their public Re•Blitz Onshape document (dfb391aac173a4555d00a5b5). It's re-expressed in the robot model's frame with its origin on the intake pivot. `js/robotModels.js` loads it in the browser and swings it about that pivot; the drawn intake stays until it loads, and headless runs never load it.

2910's parts come from a STEP export, tessellated by OpenCascade (`tools/step2glb.py`) with the surfaces' own normals, so wheels, rollers and tubes render round. A glTF export from Onshape comes pre-faceted, and the old pipeline then simplified it to 8% and dropped the normals, which is what made round parts look faceted. STEP carries the exact surfaces (not the parametric history, which stays in Onshape), so the tessellation accuracy is ours to pick: 1.2 mm and 0.45 rad here, then a light simplification that leaves hard edges alone. The STEP export flattens the subassembly tree, so `robots/2910.json` picks the parts of each file by matching name and position against the glTF export (`like`), which also says which panels are see-through. To rebuild them (the STEP translation is an async API job; the Onshape export page works too):
```
DID=dfb391aac173a4555d00a5b5 WID=3dc64f602735252892b0e47b tools/onshape-export.sh /tmp/cad/r2910.glb 6c654da4eb6b1710fb0900bd
# STEP: POST /api/v6/assemblies/d/$DID/w/$WID/e/6c654da4eb6b1710fb0900bd/translations {"formatName":"STEP","storeInDocument":false},
# poll /api/v6/translations/<id> until DONE, then GET /api/v6/documents/d/$DID/externaldata/<resultExternalDataIds[0]>
python3 -m venv /tmp/ocp && /tmp/ocp/bin/pip install cadquery-ocp
/tmp/ocp/bin/python tools/step2glb.py /tmp/cad/r2910.step /tmp/cad/r2910-step.glb --lin 0.0012 --ang 0.45 \
  --only "42 - R2 Intake" "32 - R2 Shooter" "62 - R2 Hopper" \
  --skip "screw|bolt|\bnut\b|washer|rivet|spacer|chain|belt|tensioner|bearing|gear|sprocket|kraken|motor|plug|collar|\bpin\b|hub|insert|pulley|retaining|wcp-0982|^9\d{4}a|^fuel\b|shcs|bhcs|fhcs"
node tools/extract-parts.mjs /tmp/cad/r2910-step.glb cad/robots/2910.json   # finds r2910.glb next to it for "like"
```
`robots/2910-hopper.glb` is their "R2 Hopper": its side, top and front panels sit over the shooter when stowed and slide out along the slotted rails as the intake deploys (0.27 m, `storage.extLen`). The slots allow up to ~0.48 m; the travel isn't in the export.
`robots/2910-shooter.glb` is their "Shooter & Feeder": the powered floor, the roller ramp that indexes FUEL up to the drum, and the drum at the back. It's static (origin at the robot center) and replaces the drawn tower once it loads. Its hood (their "32-17 Hood Assembly" and the two "32-06 Hood Roller Assembly" rollers: arms that pivot on the drum shaft) is split out into `robots/2910-hood.glb`, with its origin on the drum axis, so the game can swing it with the shot (`shooter.hood` in `js/robotConfigs.js`). The STEP conversion merges parts, so `tools/split-glb.mjs` picks the hood's triangles by where they are, on the surface of those subassemblies in the glTF export:

```
DID=dfb391aac173a4555d00a5b5 WID=6c9dc52fa399aa1005d464f3 tools/onshape-export.sh /tmp/cad/r2910.glb 6c654da4eb6b1710fb0900bd
node tools/split-glb.mjs cad/robots/2910-shooter.glb /tmp/cad/r2910.glb -y "32-17 Hood Assembly|32-06 Hood Roller Assembly" -0.283,0.47,0 cad/robots/2910-shooter.glb cad/robots/2910-hood.glb
```

(run it on the shooter as `tools/extract-parts.mjs` writes it; the workspace ID above is the document's current one, the export recipe above used an older one).

The X_T carries no mates, so the joints come from the geometry. `tools/step-axes.py` lists every part's cylindrical faces' axes (OpenCascade), which give each roller's, wheel's and pulley's axis, the turret's axis (the bearing), the hood's (the 4in wheel's axle: the hood's arms turn about it) and the kicker bar's pin; belts are matched to the pulleys they wrap. The Open pose is imported too ("1690-26-Top Level Open.x_t": part studio 7b7ddd375a7a915584792e39, its blob a605fe0e4c4de85f72219b5c), exported as STEP from the part studio (`POST .../partstudios/.../translations`) and read with `tools/step-axes.py`; matching each Open part to its Closed one by name and volume (nearest center of mass) gives what moves: every carriage part by one vector, 0.3196 m out and 12.5° down (its black bars' slide); the kicker bar's arms and tube 163.0° about their pin (each at the same distance from it in both: the pin checks); the floor's front five rollers 2.1° lower about the sixth; the climber (sub-assembly 600) 0.212 m up; the turret from -24° to straight ahead (the hood's angle the same). Nothing of the hopper itself, and not the chain's tray, spring, guide or swivel (posed apart from the turret). Every drive is traced from its motor through each gear, pulley and belt (README.md, *1690*: the table): tooth counts from the BOM's names, from each belt's length and the radii it wraps (its inner cylinders' axes are its pulleys': the pitch circles then check against the belt's length to 0.1 mm), and from gears' outside diameters and center distances; `tools/step-axes.py` lists the axes. The X_T doesn't include the energy chain's links (its tray, spring, wheel, guide channel and the swivel on the turret are there), so the links are laid along that route (`js/echain.js` `SWIVEL1690`); its swivel goes in `1690-turret`, the spring's wheel and carriage are drawn (they slide). Only the derived GLBs and path mesh are committed, not the X_T, the STEP or the team's renders.

Which parts are aluminum is decided part by part: `tools/aluminum-colors.py <cache>` writes each team's `<team>-materials.json` (every part's name, Onshape part id, BOM material and placements) into its cache, and a recipe's `"bom": "<team>-materials.json"` (copied next to the source GLB) has `tools/extract-parts.mjs` put the BOM's aluminum parts in materials named `alu` and its other materials' parts in `not-alu`, the geometry unchanged. The teams' CAD keeps moving (intakes dragged, turrets turned), so where a fresh export no longer reproduces a GLB exactly, `--retag` splits the GLB as it is by the nearest part of the fresh export instead; each recipe's `"source"` says which way and from which pose (a STEP translation exports only a workspace's current state, so its parts were moved to an older microversion's assembly transforms). 971's GLBs stay on the color rule: its workspace's export no longer has the subassemblies they came from. A recipe's `"bomKinds"` (604's so far) names some of the BOM's other materials finer, by regex on the material (or on the part's name, for parts it gives none): `coroplast`, `plywood`, `print`, `rubber` and `steel` are drawn as that material in the part's color (`js/robotModels.js` `KIND_LOOK`), optionally repainted (`"recolor"`: a CAD's dark gray for what's black on the robot), and `poly` as clear polycarbonate.
